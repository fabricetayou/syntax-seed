# Pica — Picandra Everwise

**The historian of programming languages.**

She remembers when Fortran was young, why a committee's beautiful monster (ALGOL 68) frightened
everyone into Pascal, how a ten-day prototype called JavaScript conquered the world, and what
languages are being born right now, in the first years of the AI era.

Pica is an intriguing, Socratic guide: she answers with precision, then adds a *detour* — a
surprising lineage, a paradox — and a question that makes you think. Ask her anything about any
language that has ever had a name.

## What she can do for you

**1. Quick-start documents.** A ready-to-run starter for ~200 languages: install, hello world,
the three first concepts to learn, where to go next.

> *"Quick start with Rust, please."*

**2. Genealogy.** Languages related to, influenced by, or descended from a given one — the whole
family tree of a language in one answer.

> *"Which languages descend from ALGOL 60?"* → almost the entire mainstream: Pascal, C, Ada, ML, Java…

**3. Scope.** Where a language is used, what paradigms it speaks, whether it is alive, thriving
or a beautiful ruin.

> *"Is COBOL dead?"* → Short answer: absolutely not. Long answer: the banking system says hello.

**4. Underlying stacks.** What a language is actually made of, layer by layer.

> *"What lies beneath TypeScript?"* → JavaScript → C++ engines → LLVM IR → machine code.

**5. The languages that never execute.** HTML, JSON, YAML, Markdown, BNF, UML — the great
descriptive family of computing: languages that only live through the applications that read them.

**6. Timelines and storytelling.** *"What was born in 1972?"* → C, Prolog, Smalltalk — and
INTERCAL, the first joke language. Pica tells history the way it happened: full of rivalry,
revival and humor.

## How she works

Pica is grounded in **it-langs**, an open structured corpus of **212 YAML fiches** (and growing):
one file per language — identity, year, creators, paradigms, typing, execution model, family,
influences, underlying stack, domains, hello world, documentation links. Pioneers (1945) to the
AI era (Mojo, Bend, Triton), including query languages, hardware description, proof assistants
and esoteric curiosities.

Through her **Computer MCP**, she reads the corpus live from its Git repository and cites the
fiches she used. If a language is not yet in the corpus, she says so plainly — she does not
invent history. She is bilingual (French/English) and speaks like a guide in a great library.

## Why she matters

- **Culture.** Programming languages are the intellectual history of our civilization's last
  eighty years — and almost nowhere is it taught as one continuous story. Pica is that story,
  queryable.
- **Education.** Quick-starts for two hundred languages, honest scope assessments, lineages that
  explain *why* languages are the way they are.
- **Craft.** Grounded answers with cited sources, structured data, and a strict no-invention
  policy on dates and creators.
- **Openness.** A free managed agent: callers use their own Pollinations balance; no walled
  knowledge.

## Roadmap — the dream

Cover **every named language since the beginning** — the long tail of regional, teaching and
experimental languages, the esoteric zoo, the DSLs — and follow, in real time, the radical turn
that AI has imposed on language design since the early 2020s. The corpus is built to grow;
every new fiche makes Pica sharper.
